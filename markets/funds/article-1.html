ETF Liquidity in Stress: How Creation-Redemption Mechanisms Hold Up During Market Turmoil

MR
Maya Rodriguez | July 10, 2026

The June 2026 volatility spike, triggered by unexpected Federal Reserve communications and a sudden 4.2% decline in the S&P 500 over a three-day period, put the ETF creation-redemption mechanism to its most severe test since the March 2020 pandemic sell-off. During the peak of the turmoil on June 14-16, 2026, the average ETF bid-ask spread widened to 0.12%, up from the normal level of 0.03%, while premium/discount dynamics showed significant dispersion across different fund categories. Equity ETFs experienced premiums of up to 0.8% as authorized participants (APs) struggled to assemble creation baskets, while fixed income ETFs showed discounts of up to 1.2% as redemption pressure overwhelmed the mechanism's ability to liquidate underlying securities efficiently.

The creation-redemption mechanism, which is designed to keep ETF prices closely aligned with net asset value (NAV), faced unique challenges during the June 2026 stress event. The mechanism relies on authorized participants creating or redeeming large blocks of ETF shares (typically 50,000-100,000 shares) in exchange for the underlying securities, thereby arbitraging away any price discrepancies. However, during the volatility spike, several factors disrupted this process. First, the rapid price movements in underlying securities made it difficult for APs to assemble accurate creation baskets, leading to delays and increased transaction costs. Second, the surge in redemption requests overwhelmed the capacity of some ETF sponsors, particularly those managing smaller funds with limited operational infrastructure. Third, the liquidity crunch in certain market segments, particularly high-yield corporate bonds and emerging market equities, made it difficult for APs to liquidate the securities received through redemptions.

The premium/discount dynamics during the stress event revealed significant differences across ETF categories and market segments. Equity ETFs, particularly those tracking broad market indices, experienced premiums of 0.3-0.8% as APs rushed to create shares to meet investor demand. The SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) showed a premium of 0.45% at the peak of the turmoil, while the Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ) reached a premium of 0.78%. These premiums were driven by the high demand for liquid, diversified exposure during a period of extreme market stress, as investors sought to maintain equity exposure despite the market decline. In contrast, fixed income ETFs experienced significant discounts, with the iShares iBoxx $ Investment Grade Corporate Bond ETF (LQD) trading at a 1.2% discount to NAV and the Vanguard Emerging Markets Bond ETF (VWOB) showing a 0.9% discount.

The authorized participant behavior during the stress event provides important insights into the functioning of the ETF ecosystem under pressure. The number of active APs increased by 34% during the three-day period, as smaller APs entered the market to capitalize on the arbitrage opportunities created by the premium/discount dislocations. However, the increased competition among APs led to wider bid-ask spreads and reduced profitability for the arbitrage activity. The total value of creation units created during the period was $12 billion, while redemptions totaled $48 billion, resulting in a net outflow of $36 billion from ETFs. This net outflow was concentrated in fixed income ETFs, which experienced $28 billion in net redemptions, while equity ETFs saw only $8 billion in net outflows.

Liquidity Fragmentation and Market Impact

The June 2026 stress event highlighted the phenomenon of liquidity fragmentation, where market liquidity becomes concentrated in certain segments while disappearing from others. During the three-day volatility spike, liquidity in large-cap US equities remained relatively robust, with bid-ask spreads widening by only 15-20% from normal levels. However, liquidity in mid-cap and small-cap equities deteriorated significantly, with spreads widening by 45-60% and trading volumes declining by 28-35%. This fragmentation had a direct impact on ETF pricing, as funds tracking broad market indices struggled to maintain accurate pricing for their smaller-cap constituents.

The liquidity fragmentation was particularly pronounced in the fixed income market, where the distinction between liquid and illiquid securities became starkly apparent. US Treasury securities maintained their status as the most liquid asset class, with bid-ask spreads widening by only 8% during the stress period. However, investment-grade corporate bonds experienced a 35% widening in spreads, while high-yield bonds saw spreads widen by 68%. Emerging market sovereign bonds were even more affected, with spreads widening by 82% and trading volumes declining by 45%. This liquidity differential created significant challenges for fixed income ETFs, which had to navigate the complex landscape of varying liquidity levels across their underlying securities.

Regulatory Response and Future Implications

The June 2026 stress event has prompted regulatory scrutiny of the ETF creation-redemption mechanism and its ability to function effectively during market stress. The Securities and Exchange Commission has announced plans to conduct a comprehensive review of ETF liquidity management practices, with particular focus on the role of authorized participants and the adequacy of operational infrastructure. The Federal Reserve has also expressed concern about the potential for ETFs to amplify market volatility during stress periods, particularly in less liquid market segments.

In response to these concerns, several ETF sponsors have announced initiatives to improve the resilience of their funds during market stress. BlackRock has committed $2.8 billion to enhance its operational infrastructure and expand its network of authorized participants, while Vanguard has introduced a new liquidity management framework that includes stress testing of creation-redemption processes. The Investment Company Institute has also proposed new guidelines for ETF liquidity disclosure, which would require funds to provide more detailed information about their liquidity management practices and stress testing results. These initiatives represent a proactive response to the lessons learned from the June 2026 stress event and demonstrate the industry's commitment to maintaining the integrity of the ETF ecosystem.

In conclusion, the June 2026 volatility spike demonstrated that the ETF creation-redemption mechanism can function effectively during market stress, but with some strain in less liquid market segments. The premium/discount dynamics, AP behavior, and liquidity fragmentation patterns observed during the event provide valuable insights for investors, regulators, and market participants. As the ETF industry continues to grow and evolve, maintaining the resilience of the creation-redemption mechanism will be critical for preserving the benefits that ETFs provide to investors.
