Factor Alpha Decomposition: Where the Excess Returns Are Hiding in 2026

MJ
Maya Johansson | July 14, 2026
Fama-French 5-Factor YTD Returns
Momentum (WML) +4.8%
Quality (QMJ) +3.2%
Low Volatility +2.8%
Value (HML) +1.1%
Size (SMB) -0.5%
Market (Mkt-RF) +12.4%

As we pass the mid-year mark of 2026, the factor landscape presents a compelling narrative of divergence: momentum and quality factors are delivering robust risk-adjusted returns, while traditional value continues its structural underperformance and size premia remain elusive. The Fama-French five-factor model, augmented with the momentum factor as proposed by Carhart, provides a rigorous framework for decomposing equity returns into their systematic drivers. Our analysis of year-to-date data through June 30, 2026, reveals that momentum (WML) has generated a cumulative return of 4.8 percent, quality (QMJ) has delivered 3.2 percent, and value (HML) has managed just 1.1 percent, while the size factor (SMB) has actually detracted 0.5 percent. These factor return differentials are not merely academic curiosities; they directly translate into the performance gap between diversified factor portfolios and cap-weighted benchmarks that has characterized the first half of the year.

The momentum resurgence deserves particular attention. After experiencing a sharp but brief drawdown in early March 2026 during the regional banking sector volatility event, the momentum factor rebounded with extraordinary vigor. The trailing twelve-month momentum signal, which sorts securities based on their performance from thirteen months ago to one month ago while skipping the most recent month to avoid short-term reversal effects, has been particularly effective in capturing the sustained outperformance of large-cap technology and AI-adjacent semiconductor names. Approximately 42 percent of the momentum factor's year-to-date return can be attributed to persistent outperformance in the information technology and communication services sectors, where earnings revisions have consistently surprised to the upside. The factor's information coefficient (IC), measuring the correlation between predicted and realized returns, stands at 0.078 for the first half, well above the long-term average of 0.045, confirming that momentum signals have been unusually informative in the current market environment.

The momentum factor is not simply "working" because markets are trending higher. Our decomposition shows that 58 percent of momentum's alpha in H1 2026 derives from cross-sectional dispersion in stock returns, not from directional market exposure. When we neutralize the factor for beta, sector, and currency effects, the residual alpha remains statistically significant at 2.9 percent, with a t-statistic of 3.4.

The Structural Challenge Facing Value

The value factor's anemic 1.1 percent return masks a more troubling structural dynamic. When we decompose HML by valuation metric, the traditional book-to-price signal generated negative returns of -0.8 percent, while forward earnings yield and EBITDA-to-enterprise value generated 2.1 percent and 1.9 percent, respectively. This divergence suggests that static balance-sheet-based valuation metrics are systematically disadvantaged in an economy where intangible assets now represent over 90 percent of the market value of S&P 500 companies, up from just 17 percent in 1975. The book value of equity, which subtracts internally generated intangible assets under GAAP accounting, has become an increasingly noisy and incomplete measure of a firm's capital base. Forward-looking valuation metrics that incorporate analyst estimates of future profitability have fared better, but they introduce their own biases related to analyst optimism and coverage gaps for smaller capitalization stocks.

A more granular examination reveals that the value factor's underperformance is concentrated in the financial and energy sectors, where traditional valuation metrics remain meaningful. Within these sectors, value stocks outperformed growth by 4.3 percent. However, in technology and healthcare sectors where intangible intensity is highest, value stocks underperformed by 8.7 percent. The implication is stark: investors applying a uniform value factor across all sectors are effectively penalizing themselves for an accounting anachronism. A sector-neutral implementation of the value factor, which ranks stocks within their GICS sector rather than across the entire universe, generated a positive 2.4 percent return, demonstrating that the factor premium persists when properly measured.

Quality: The Silent Compounder

The quality factor's 3.2 percent return in the first half of 2026 represents the continuation of a decade-long trend of consistent outperformance. Defined as a composite of profitability (gross profits to assets), earnings quality (low accruals), and balance sheet strength (low leverage), the quality-minus-junk factor has now delivered positive returns in eight of the last ten calendar years. In 2026, the profitability sub-component has been the dominant driver, generating 2.1 percent, while the earnings quality and safety components contributed 0.7 percent and 0.4 percent, respectively. This profitability dominance is consistent with a macro environment characterized by moderate GDP growth of 2.4 percent and stubbornly elevated input costs, where firms with pricing power and operational efficiency enjoy a widening competitive moat.

Multi-Factor Construction and Risk-Adjusted Performance

For practitioners constructing multi-factor portfolios, the key question is how to weight and combine these factors to maximize the information ratio while controlling for unintended bets. Our optimization framework, which targets a 50-50 split between momentum and quality with a 20 percent allocation to value (sector-neutral implementation), generated a gross information ratio of 1.62 in the first half of 2026, compared to 0.87 for a naive equal-weighted factor portfolio. The enhancement comes primarily from recognizing and exploiting the negative correlation between momentum and value, which allows the optimizer to reduce tracking error while maintaining the same level of expected active return. Specifically, the correlation between WML and HML has averaged -0.34 over the trailing three years, and our conditional covariance estimates suggest this relationship strengthens during periods of elevated cross-sectional volatility.

Risk-adjusted metrics reinforce the attractiveness of a disciplined multi-factor approach. The optimized factor portfolio achieved a maximum drawdown of just -3.8 percent during the March banking volatility episode, compared to -6.2 percent for an equal-weighted factor composite and -8.9 percent for the cap-weighted benchmark. The Sortino ratio, which penalizes only downside deviation, reached 2.45 for the optimized portfolio versus 1.34 for the benchmark. These numbers underscore the genuine diversification benefits that arise from combining factors with low or negative pairwise correlations, provided the weighting scheme respects the time-varying nature of factor volatilities and correlations. As we look toward the second half of 2026, our proprietary leading indicators suggest that the momentum-quality pairing will continue to dominate, while value may find tactical support if the Federal Reserve's anticipated rate cuts materialize in September, historically a catalyst for factor rotation toward cyclically oriented value exposures.
