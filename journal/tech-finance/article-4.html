High-Frequency Trading Infrastructure: Latency Arms Race in 2026

ZO
Dr. Zara Okafor | Head of Fintech Research | March 18, 2026
Average Latency 8 µs
Colocation Cost $25K/mo
FPGA Adoption 45%
Microwave Network Coverage 78%
Market Maker Share 52%
Annual Infrastructure Spend $3.8B

The latency arms race in electronic trading shows no signs of abating. Average round-trip latency at major US equity exchanges has compressed to 8 microseconds, down from 17 microseconds in 2022, driven by a combination of FPGA acceleration, microwave network expansion, and exchange infrastructure upgrades. Total annual infrastructure spending by high-frequency trading firms and market makers has reached $3.8 billion, underscoring the capital intensity of competing at the technological frontier of modern markets.

The primary drivers of latency reduction have shifted over the past four years. While fiber optic improvements and exchange matching engine upgrades delivered marginal gains in the early 2020s, the current wave of improvement comes from three converging technology vectors: field-programmable gate array deployment for order processing, microwave and millimeter-wave wireless networks for inter-exchange communication, and specialized network interface cards with kernel bypass capabilities.

FPGA vs. GPU Acceleration

FPGA adoption among HFT firms has climbed to 45%, up from 28% in 2023, as the technology matures beyond niche applications into mainstream trading infrastructure. FPGAs offer deterministic latency measured in nanoseconds and the ability to implement custom trading logic directly in hardware. In contrast, GPU acceleration, while dominant in backtesting and signal research, remains suboptimal for production execution due to higher latency variability. The emerging frontier is heterogeneous computing architectures that pair FPGAs for execution with GPUs for real-time signal generation, connected via high-bandwidth interconnects.

Microwave vs. Fiber: The Network Battle

Microwave networks now cover 78% of the most liquid US trading routes, including the critical Chicago-New Jersey corridor where signal propagation through air offers a 3.5-microsecond advantage over fiber optic cables following the curvature of the earth. The economics of microwave deployment favor the largest market makers, with a single Chicago-New Jersey link costing approximately $15 million to construct and $800,000 annually to maintain. This creates a structural barrier to entry that reinforces the dominance of established players.

Exchange Colocation Economics

Colocation costs have risen 40% since 2022, with premium rack space adjacent to major exchange matching engines now commanding $25,000 per month. The largest HFT firms maintain colocation presence across 12-18 venues globally, representing annual infrastructure commitments exceeding $5 million before any trading occurs. This cost structure effectively sets a minimum efficient scale for HFT operations, contributing to industry consolidation where the top five firms now account for 52% of market-making volume in US equities.

Market Quality and Regulatory Response

The impact of HFT infrastructure investment on market quality remains contested. Bid-ask spreads in S&P 500 constituents have narrowed to an average of 0.82 basis points, a measurable benefit attributable to automated market making. However, the EU's MiFID III framework, expected to take effect in 2027, introduces new requirements for minimum resting times, fee structure transparency, and real-time monitoring of quote-to-trade ratios that could reshape the economics of latency-sensitive strategies. The US SEC is closely monitoring European developments as it considers its own market structure reforms.

Key Insight: The HFT latency arms race has entered a phase of diminishing returns where each microsecond of improvement costs exponentially more than the last. The real competitive advantage is shifting from pure speed to the quality of signal processing — firms that combine FPGAs for execution with advanced AI for alpha generation will define the next era.
