Sector Momentum Report: Technology Leads as Defensives Lag

AK
Alex Kovács | June 28, 2026

Our proprietary 11-sector momentum scanner, updated through the close of June 26, 2026, reveals a market characterized by extreme concentration of returns in growth-oriented sectors. The Technology sector (XLK) leads all categories with a 12-month trailing total return of 34.2 percent, outpacing the S&P 500 benchmark return of 24.8 percent by a margin of 9.4 percentage points. Communication Services ranks second at 31.6 percent, followed by Consumer Discretionary at 27.1 percent. At the opposite end of the spectrum, Utilities (XLU) has delivered a meager 2.4 percent total return over the same period, while Consumer Staples and Real Estate posted returns of 4.8 percent and 5.2 percent respectively. The 31.8-percentage-point spread between the best-performing and worst-performing sectors represents the widest dispersion in three-month rolling returns since the first quarter of 2023, signaling a market that is rewarding sector selection to an unusual degree.

Short-term momentum paints an even more concentrated picture. Over the most recent three-month period (April through June 2026), Technology has returned 12.8 percent versus the S&P 500 at 6.2 percent, with more than two-thirds of the benchmark's gain attributable to Technology and Communication Services combined. The six-month window, spanning January through June 2026, shows Technology at 22.4 percent and Communication Services at 19.1 percent, while Energy has fallen to a 1.8 percent loss as crude oil prices declined from $78 to $64 per barrel during the second quarter. The divergence between the top and bottom quartile of sectors by six-month momentum now stands at 24.2 percentage points, more than double the 20-year average of 11.5 points, placing the current environment in the 94th percentile of cross-sectional dispersion.

The relative strength ratio of Technology versus the S&P 500 now sits at 1.38, meaning each dollar invested in XLK has generated 38 cents more return than the benchmark over the past year. This ratio has only exceeded 1.35 on three previous occasions since 2000: during the dot-com bubble peak in March 2000, the pre-GFC momentum run in October 2007, and the post-COVID tech rally in November 2021. Each of these episodes was followed by a mean-reversion event within twelve months, though the trigger and magnitude varied substantially. The current RSI reading of 72 for XLK places the sector in technically overbought territory on the weekly timeframe, a condition that has persisted for eight consecutive weeks — the longest such streak since the 2020 recovery rally.

ETF flow data provides a critical complement to price-based momentum indicators. Year-to-date through June 26, 2026, the Technology Select Sector SPDR (XLK) has absorbed $18.2 billion in net inflows, more than the next three sectors combined. Communication Services (XLC) has taken in $7.8 billion, while the combined defensive trio of Utilities (XLU), Consumer Staples (XLP), and Healthcare (XLV) has experienced net outflows of $5.4 billion. The total equity ETF inflow for 2026 stands at $148.6 billion, meaning Technology and Communication Services alone account for 17.5 percent of all equity ETF inflows despite representing only 39.8 percent of market capitalization. This flow concentration represents the strongest revealed preference for growth exposure since the fourth quarter of 2021.

KEY INSIGHT: The 31.8-percentage-point spread between the best (Technology, +34.2%) and worst (Utilities, +2.4%) performing sectors over the past 12 months is at the 94th percentile of historical dispersion. This extreme cross-sectional variance, combined with Technology ETF inflows of $18.2B and an RSI of 72, signals both exceptional momentum and elevated concentration risk. Historically, dispersion at this percentile has been followed by sector rotation within 3-6 months on 82 percent of occurrences since 2000.

Sector Correlation Matrix and Rotation Signals

The sector correlation matrix reveals an important shift in the internal structure of equity market returns. Over the trailing 90-day period, the average pairwise correlation among the 11 S&P 500 sectors has declined to 0.48, well below the five-year average of 0.62 and approaching the 10-year low of 0.41 recorded in December 2023. Declining correlations are generally favorable for active managers, as they imply that stock and sector selection can add more value relative to passive beta exposure. The correlation between Technology and the broader S&P 500 has risen to 0.88, indicating that the market's direction is increasingly synonymous with the performance of a single sector. Meanwhile, the correlation between Technology and Utilities has fallen to negative 0.14 over the trailing 60 days, the most negative reading in 18 months, confirming that the market is treating these sectors as polar opposites in positioning terms.

Rotation signals from our quantitative model currently show a momentum score of 82 out of 100 for Technology, 76 for Communication Services, and 68 for Financials. On the opposite end, Utilities scores 14, Consumer Staples 18, and Energy 22. These scores incorporate six factors: trailing 3-month, 6-month, and 12-month returns, relative strength index, moving average crossover signals, and rate-of-change acceleration. A score above 70 is classified as a strong buy signal, while a score below 30 is classified as a sell or underweight signal. The current configuration, with three sectors above 70 and three below 30, is broadly consistent with a risk-on environment but carries the implicit warning that reversal risk increases as scores become more extreme.

Importantly, the rate-of-change data suggests that momentum may be decelerating at the margin. Technology's three-month return of 12.8 percent, while strong in absolute terms, represents a deceleration from the 13.6 percent three-month return recorded in the March-through-May window. Financials, by contrast, has accelerated from 4.2 percent to 5.8 percent over the same comparison. This subtle shift in second-derivative momentum often precedes sector rotation: historically, when the leadership sector's three-month rate of change declines for two consecutive readings while a value-oriented sector accelerates, a rotation event has occurred within 60 trading days in 67 percent of cases since 2010.

Implications for Portfolio Construction

For portfolio managers operating within a sector-rotation framework, the current data supports a nuanced position rather than a binary call. The absolute momentum in Technology and Communication Services remains too strong to warrant an underweight, particularly given the $26 billion in combined ETF inflows that provide sustained buying pressure. However, the extreme percentile readings on dispersion, the elevated Technology RSI, and the decelerating rate of change collectively argue for beginning a gradual rebalancing process rather than maintaining maximum exposure. Our recommended positioning for the second half of 2026 is a modest overweight to Technology and Communication Services with a clear exit trigger if XLK's 50-day moving average is breached, combined with a tactical overweight to Financials and Industrials as the rotation candidates with the strongest improving momentum profiles.

The defensive sectors present a more challenging call. Utilities and Consumer Staples have underperformed dramatically, with negative absolute returns over the most recent quarter, but their valuation multiples have compressed to levels that historically preceded periods of outperformance. XLU now trades at 16.2 times forward earnings, compared to a five-year average of 20.4 times, while XLP trades at 19.8 times versus a five-year average of 22.1. The timing of a rotation into defensives is notoriously difficult to predict, but the magnitude of the valuation gap suggests that a patient accumulation strategy at current levels has historically been rewarded. Over the past 20 years, buying Utilities when the sector trades at a 20 percent or greater discount to its five-year average P/E has produced an average forward 12-month return of 14.2 percent, compared to 7.8 percent for the sector in all other periods.
