Healthcare Insurance Litigation: Denial-of-Coverage Legal Strategies

MC
Michael Chen, JD|Jun 18, 2026

Healthcare insurance denial-of-coveragelitigation represents one of the fastest-growing areas of healthcare law, with the number of coverage dispute cases increasing by 34% in the past two years. As insurance companies implement more sophisticated algorithms to evaluate claims, patients and healthcare providers are increasingly challenging denials through both administrative appeals and litigation.

The most common grounds for coverage denial litigation include medical necessity disputes, experimental treatment exclusions, network adequacy challenges, and ERISA-governed plan interpretations. Courts have increasingly adopted a treating physician standard, giving significant weight to the recommendations of the patient's treating physician over the opinions of insurance company medical directors.

ERISA-governed health plans present unique litigation challenges due to federal preemption of state insurance laws. However, recent judicial interpretations have created exceptions that allow patients to pursue bad faith claims against ERISA plan administrators who demonstrate a pattern of arbitrary and capricious denials.

KEY INSIGHT: The treating physician standard is gaining traction in coverage dispute litigation. Patients and providers should document clinical rationale thoroughly, exhaust administrative appeals, and consider class action strategies for systematic denial patterns.

For patients and providers navigating coverage disputes, a strategic approach is essential. First, thoroughly document the medical necessity of the treatment. Second, exhaust all administrative appeal processes before pursuing litigation. Third, consider engaging a healthcare attorney who specializes in insurance litigation. Fourth, evaluate the potential for class action or coordinated litigation if the denial reflects a systematic practice.
