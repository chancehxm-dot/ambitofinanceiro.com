Telemedicine Legal Framework: Cross-State Practice and Licensure

RT
Rebecca Torres, JD|Jun 2, 2026

Telemedicine has transformed healthcare delivery, but the legal framework governing cross-state practice remains a patchwork of inconsistent state regulations. While the COVID-19 pandemic prompted temporary relaxations of telehealth licensure requirements, most states have reverted to pre-pandemic rules or adopted modified frameworks that still require providers to hold licenses in the state where the patient is located.

The Interstate Medical Licensure Compact (IMLC) has emerged as a partial solution, with 42 states currently participating. However, the IMLC does not eliminate the requirement for separate state licenses — it merely expedites the application process.

Liability exposure in telemedicine presents unique challenges. When a physician provides telemedicine services to a patient in another state, the applicable standard of care is generally determined by the state where the patient is located — not the state where the physician practices.

KEY INSIGHT: Telemedicine providers must adopt a state-by-state compliance approach. The IMLC simplifies but does not eliminate multi-state compliance obligations. Liability insurance must cover cross-state practice.

Telehealth parity laws have been adopted by 38 states, requiring insurance companies to cover telemedicine services at the same rate as in-person visits. However, the specifics vary significantly across jurisdictions, creating compliance challenges for providers operating across state lines.
