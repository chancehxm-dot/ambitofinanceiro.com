Malpractice Case Outcomes: A 10-Year Longitudinal Study

RC
Dr. Robert Chen, PhD|Jul 10, 2026

This longitudinal study examines 12,400 medical malpractice cases filed between 2016 and 2025, analyzing factors that influence litigation outcomes. The study reveals significant trends in malpractice patterns, settlement amounts, and verdict outcomes with important implications for providers and policymakers.

Clinical specialty is the strongest predictor of malpractice risk, with surgical specialties accounting for 34% of all claims despite representing only 18% of practicing physicians. Obstetrics and neurosurgery consistently demonstrate the highest per-physician claim rates.

Total Cases Analyzed12,400
Surgical Claims34%
Avg Settlement$342,000
Plaintiff Win Rate28%

The average malpractice settlement increased from $268,000 in 2016 to $342,000 in 2025, a 28% increase. Cases involving severe patient harm accounted for 68% of total settlement value despite representing only 23% of cases.

KEY INSIGHT: 62% of malpractice claims involve communication failure. Investing in communication training and documentation practices is the most cost-effective risk management strategy.

AI-assisted diagnostics emerged as a factor in later study years, with AI-related claims increasing from 2.1% to 14.2%. Healthcare organizations with AI governance frameworks demonstrate lower malpractice exposure in AI-related cases.
