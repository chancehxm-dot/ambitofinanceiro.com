The Cost of Healthcare Litigation: Economic Impact on Providers

PW
Dr. Patricia Wells, PhD|Jun 15, 2026

The economic burden of healthcare litigation extends far beyond direct legal costs. This study quantifies the full economic impact including direct costs, indirect costs, and opportunity costs across 3,200 healthcare providers from 2021 to 2025.

Direct costs — legal fees, settlements, damages — average $46,300 per physician per year. Surgeons face the highest costs at $78,400, while primary care physicians face $18,200. Malpractice insurance premiums have increased 8.2% annually.

Avg Direct Cost/Physician$46,300/yr
Surgeon Direct Cost$78,400/yr
Defensive Medicine Cost$46B/yr
Total Economic Burden$82B/yr

Defensive medicine costs the US healthcare system approximately $46 billion annually. 73% of physicians acknowledge ordering defensive medicine, with 21% of clinical decisions influenced by malpractice concerns.

KEY INSIGHT: The total economic burden is $82 billion annually — nearly eight times direct costs. Tort reform could reduce this by $18 billion annually while maintaining access to justice.

Physicians in active malpractice cases spend 12.4% of professional time on litigation activities. States with comprehensive tort reform demonstrate 22% lower per-physician litigation costs. The study recommends damage caps, alternative dispute resolution, and clinical practice guidelines.
