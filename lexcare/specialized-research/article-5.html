Bioethics and the Law: Navigating End-of-Life Care Decisions

MC
Dr. Margaret Collins, PhD|Jun 5, 2026

End-of-life care decisions represent one of the most legally and ethically complex areas of healthcare. This study examines advance directive frameworks, physician-assisted dying statutes, and legal mechanisms available to patients, families, and providers.

Advance directive completion rates remain alarmingly low at 37% of adults. Among those who have completed directives, 52% fail to address key scenarios such as dementia care or artificial nutrition.

Physician-assisted dying laws have expanded to 12 states and DC. These laws are associated with 23% higher palliative care utilization in states that have enacted them.

KEY INSIGHT: Healthcare organizations must develop comprehensive policies addressing advance directive management, palliative care integration, and family communication. Proactive advance care planning reduces legal risk while honoring patient autonomy.

End-of-life litigation increased 18% year-over-year. Organizations with formal policies, ethics committees, and structured family communication experience 34% fewer claims. The study recommends proactive advance care planning, palliative care integration, and ethics committee establishment.
